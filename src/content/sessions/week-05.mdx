## The case, or the argument

The Rocketdyne F-1 powered the Saturn V's first stage. Decades after
production ended, NASA held the original drawings and still could not
straightforwardly rebuild the engine from them. Engineers went back to
physical hardware — including flight-hardware injector plates recovered
from the ocean floor and museum-held engines such as the one now in the
Smithsonian's collection — and reverse-engineered details the drawings
alone didn't settle: tolerances, weld sequences, and manufacturing
judgement calls that were never the kind of thing a drawing records.

Read this against week 4. FOGBANK's records survived and the material still
couldn't be remade from them; the F-1's records survived and the engine
still had to be partly reconstructed from the object itself, not the
paperwork about the object. Two institutions, two different missing pieces,
the same shape of gap.

## What we do in the seminar

Compare the F-1 case to FOGBANK point by point: what kind of thing was
missing in each (a material property, a manufacturing judgement, a person),
and what kind of artefact ended up substituting for the missing record (an
oversight report, a museum object). Is "the record" always a document?

## A question left open

[Reinstatement](/glossary/#reinstatement) here meant reverse-engineering
from an artefact rather than rebuilding a process from people. Does that
count as recovering the same capability that was lost, or a different one
that produces the same output?
