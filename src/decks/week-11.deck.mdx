## the case for a loss in progress

Generative models can now produce competent first drafts of writing,
code, and analysis that a person used to have to produce themselves in
order to develop the underlying judgement. Weeks 6 and 7 both describe how
a capability can erode without anyone deciding to erode it: automate the
output well enough, and the practice that used to build the underlying
skill stops happening as often.

If that pattern holds here, the [tacit
knowledge](/glossary/#tacit-knowledge) built by doing the work yourself is
exactly the kind of thing week 2 argues doesn't survive in the output
alone — and the output is increasingly all that gets produced.

---

## the case against

Every automation this course has studied changed what skill was needed
without necessarily reducing the total skill in the system — Zuboff's
argument is precisely that smart machines demand new capabilities from
the people who work with them, even as they remove old ones. Editing,
prompting, and judging model output at scale may be a real capability
being built right now, not a gap opening up.

On this reading, week 1's distinction cuts the other way: the record
(what the model produces) and the capability (what the person using it
can actually do) are just as separable in the optimistic direction as in
the pessimistic one.

```notes
Read both framing slides in the same flat tone — the deck should not tip
its hand toward either side, and the room will notice if it does.
```

## activity: split the room and debate

**Setup (5 min).** Split the room in half.

**Round 1 (15 min).** One half argues the case for a loss in progress; the
other argues the case against. Use only material from weeks 1–10, cited by
week number.

**Round 2 (15 min).** Now argue against your own side from round 1, using
the same rule: weeks 1–10 only, cited by week number.

**Reflection (5 min).** In your original pair, say one thing that changed
for you between arguing your side and arguing against it.

```notes
No side is graded as the "correct" one, because this page doesn't have
one. If a group asks which side is right, the honest answer is that the
exercise isn't built to produce that answer.
```

---

## debrief

What came out of arguing against your own side in round 2 that didn't
come up in round 1? Does the case you found more natural to argue first
still feel more natural now?

---

## wrap-up

This whole seminar is the open question. Decide, if you want to, on your
own time — but notice which case you reached for first, and what that
says about which side you were already inclined to argue. Week 12 has no
new case at all: the class brings its own, and tests whether the method
built across these eleven weeks still holds up.
