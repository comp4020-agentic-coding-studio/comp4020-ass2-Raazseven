## several labs, one set of papers

In the early 1960s, several physics labs tried to build a TEA laser from
the published literature alone. The papers were not wrong, not incomplete
in any way a referee would have flagged, and not withheld.

```notes
Sit with "not wrong, not incomplete, not withheld" for a second — the
room's first instinct will be to assume the papers were somehow at fault.
That instinct is exactly what this week is against.
```

---

## what actually predicted success

Harry Collins, studying the attempts, found success correlated with one
thing: contact, direct or one step removed, with a lab that already had a
working laser. Papers travelled freely. The ability to build the thing
they described did not.

---

## never on the record to begin with

This is the sharpest version of [tacit
knowledge](/glossary/#tacit-knowledge) the course will use: not a
capability that was lost, but one that was never on the record to begin
with. There is no rupture point in this week's diagram, because nothing
here ever broke.

```notes
Point back at week 1's legend slide: the record track and the capability
track were simply never the same track. Week 1's distinction is the
reason that sentence is coherent instead of confusing.
```

## activity: write the unwritable instructions

**Setup (5 min).** Pick a skill you have that you could not fully hand to
a stranger in writing — tying a specific knot, a debugging habit, a way
of reading a room.

**Pairs (20 min).** Write instructions for it in five minutes. Trade with
a partner and try to follow each other's instructions literally. Note the
exact line where it breaks down.

**Share-back (15 min).** One breakdown per pair, read aloud.

```notes
The breakdown line is the point of the exercise, not the instructions
themselves — steer pairs toward naming it precisely rather than polishing
the instructions to avoid it.
```

---

## debrief

Compare what went missing across the room's instructions against what
Collins found missing from the laser papers. Group the misses into kinds:
a physical calibration nobody thinks to state, a judgement call, a
corrective for when it goes slightly wrong.

---

## wrap-up

Some knowledge was never on the record to begin with — the gap here isn't
a documentation failure, it's a different kind of thing than a document
can hold. Week 3 flips this failure around: a record that is complete and
still doesn't transfer. Week 4 gives you a case built to test today's open
question directly.
