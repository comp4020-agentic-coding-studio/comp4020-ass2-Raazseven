## the claim

A production line stopped. The paperwork survived. The material could not
be reliably remade from it.

```notes
Say the claim once, plainly, before any timeline. It's the whole seminar
in one sentence, and everything after this just supports it.
```

## the shape of the timeline

- production runs, for years, without incident
- the line stops; staff move on or retire
- the material is needed again
- specifications and procedures are located, intact
- reliable reproduction does not follow from having them

## discussion

- Read against weeks 2 and 3 — is this a missing-paperwork case, or
  something else?
- Public reporting says some part of what the original production
  depended on was never captured in the documentation. What kind of thing
  does that leave uncaptured, in institutional terms — a person, a
  habit, a judgement made on the line?
- Why does institutional and financial oversight reporting count as
  evidence here, when technical description isn't something this deck is
  allowed to use?
- Week 8 has a case where a lapsed capability was reinstated. What made
  reinstatement possible there, and does anything in this week's case
  suggest why this one took years and tens of millions of dollars rather
  than months?

## activity: audit the timeline

**Setup (5 min).** As a class, walk back through the framing and diagram
slides once more, aloud, slide by slide.

**Groups (20 min).** In small groups, mark the earliest point on the
timeline where an outside auditor, reading only the records available at
that time, could plausibly have flagged the risk that reinstatement would
be this hard.

**Share-back (15 min).** Compare each group's marked point, then check it
against what the government's own reporting says actually happened.

```notes
[U.S. GAO (2008)](/readings/#gao-2008-fogbank) is the anchor citation for
this whole week — have it open during share-back, not just before the
seminar.
```

---

## debrief

How far ahead of the actual need did the earliest plausible audit point
sit, across the room's answers? What kind of signal would an auditor have
needed to see — a staffing gap, a retirement, a production lapse with no
resumption plan — none of it technical, all of it institutional?

---

## wrap-up

A record track that survives; a capability track that stops anyway. Week
5 gives you a different missing piece behind the same shape of gap. Week
8 gives you a case where reinstatement worked, to read back against this
one's cost.
