## The case, or the argument

In the early 1960s, several physics labs tried to build a TEA laser from the
published literature alone. The papers were not wrong, not incomplete in any
way a referee would have flagged, and not withheld. Harry Collins, studying
the attempts, found that success correlated with one thing: contact, direct
or one step removed, with a lab that already had a working laser. Papers
travelled freely. The ability to build the thing they described did not.

This is the sharpest version of [tacit knowledge](/glossary/#tacit-knowledge)
the course will use: not a capability that was lost, but one that was never
on the record to begin with. There is no rupture point in this week's
diagram, because nothing here ever broke — the record track and the
capability track were simply never the same track, and week 1's distinction
is the reason that sentence is coherent instead of confusing.

## What we do in the seminar

Pick a skill you have that you could not fully hand to a stranger in
writing — tying a specific knot, a debugging habit, a way of reading a
room. Try to write instructions for it in five minutes. Compare what's
missing against what Collins found missing from the laser papers.

## One reading

[Collins (1974)](/readings/#collins-1974) — read the laser case at full
length before the seminar; the paper's argument is broader than the one
case, and this week only uses the narrow version of it.

## A question left open

If some knowledge is tacit by its nature — never fully write-downable — is
"we should have documented it better" ever a complete answer to a lost
capability? Keep this open; week 4 gives you a case to test it against.
