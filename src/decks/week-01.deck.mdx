## two facts wearing the same word

An organisation can hold every drawing, every specification, every report
it ever produced about how it once did something, and still be unable to
do that thing. That sounds like a contradiction the first time you hear
it. It isn't one — it's just two different facts wearing the same word.

```notes
Let the room sit with the apparent contradiction for a second before
resolving it on the next slide. The resolution lands harder if they've
felt the confusion first.
```

---

## "we know how we did it"

- can mean: **we have the file**
- can mean: **we could do it again tomorrow**

Most institutions cannot tell you which one they mean until the
capability is needed and turns out not to be there.

---

## a method, tested eleven times

This course is a method for telling the two apart, tested against eleven
cases where an institution found out the hard way. The subject is
[institutional memory](/glossary/#institutional-memory) at the scale of an
organisation — not your own habits, not your own memory.

```notes
Name the scope explicitly: this is not a course about personal
forgetfulness. Someone will try to make it personal in discussion; that's
fine in the activity, but the claim itself is institutional.
```

## discussion

- Pick a system you know well. Is "we know how it works" there a **file**
  or a **doing**, for you specifically?
  {/* facilitate: ask for one concrete example each before opening the floor */}
- Could you tell which one your organisation means, before being asked?
  {/* facilitate: a show of hands usually splits the room here */}
- What would count as evidence you actually *have* the capability, not
  just the record of it?
- If a capability and its record can come apart, what would it take to
  notice the gap *before* you need the capability and find it isn't
  there?
  {/* facilitate: this is the question the seminar leaves open — don't answer it yet, just let it sit */}

## activity: guess documentation vs capability

**Setup (5 min).** Hand out the one-line titles of weeks 2–12, with no
case detail. Each student privately guesses, in one sentence,
"documentation problem" or "capability problem" for each.

**Pairs (20 min).** Compare guesses with a partner. Where you disagree,
argue it out — the reasoning matters more than the label you land on.

**Share-back (15 min).** A show of hands per case, tallied on the board.

```notes
Keep every guess somewhere the class can find it again — week 12 comes
back and asks whether the guessing got easier.
```

---

## debrief

Look at whichever case split the room most in the share-back. What did
the disagreement actually turn on — a fact nobody had yet, or a genuine
ambiguity in the distinction itself?

{/* facilitate: write the tally on the board and photograph it; it's the callback week 12 needs */}

## wrap-up

A capability and its record can come apart, and most institutions can't
tell you which one they're holding until it's too late to matter. Week 2
gives the sharpest version of this: a capability that was never on the
record to begin with.
