## a familiar shape, decades later

The Rocketdyne F-1 powered the Saturn V's first stage. Decades after
production ended, NASA held the original drawings and still could not
straightforwardly rebuild the engine from them.

```notes
Ask, before revealing anything else: given what week 4 argued, what would
the room predict was missing from the drawings? Let a few guesses land
before the next slide.
```

---

## going back to the object itself

Engineers went back to physical hardware — including flight-hardware
injector plates recovered from the ocean floor and museum-held engines
such as the one now in the Smithsonian's collection — and reverse-engineered
details the drawings alone didn't settle: tolerances, weld sequences, and
manufacturing judgement calls that were never the kind of thing a drawing
records.

---

## two institutions, two missing pieces

Read this against week 4. FOGBANK's records survived and the material
still couldn't be remade from them; the F-1's records survived and the
engine still had to be partly reconstructed from the object itself, not
the paperwork about the object. Same shape of gap, different missing
piece.

## discussion

- What kind of thing was missing here — a material property, a
  manufacturing judgement, a person — compared to what was missing in
  FOGBANK's case?
- What artefact ended up substituting for the missing record — an
  oversight report, a museum object? Is "the record" always a document?
- Reinstatement here meant reverse-engineering from an artefact rather
  than rebuilding a process from people. Is that the same capability
  recovered, or a different one that happens to produce the same output?

## activity: compare F-1 to FOGBANK

**Setup (5 min).** Pull up both this week's diagram and week 4's,
side by side.

**Groups (20 min).** Build a two-column comparison: for each case, what
kind of thing was missing, and what artefact or report ended up
substituting for it.

**Share-back (15 min).** Each group presents one row of the comparison;
compile a shared table on the board.

```notes
Push groups past "both had a gap" toward naming the specific *kind* of
missing thing in each — that specificity is what the debrief needs.
```

---

## debrief

Look at the compiled table. Does "the record" always end up being a
document in the end, or did one or both cases resolve through something
that wasn't a document at all?

---

## wrap-up

Two institutions, two different missing pieces, the same shape of gap —
and in neither case did having the paperwork turn out to be enough. Week
6 changes the pattern entirely: a capability given away on purpose,
rather than lost by accident.
