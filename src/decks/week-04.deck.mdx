# FOGBANK

## a record track that survives, a capability track that stops

{/* _class: impact */}

---

## the claim

A production line stopped. The paperwork survived. The material could not
be reliably remade from it.

```notes
Say the claim once, plainly, before any timeline. It's the whole slide deck
in one sentence, and everything after this just supports it.
```

---

## what this deck will not do

- no materials detail
- no process detail
- no engineering detail

Institutional and documentary history only: timeline, staffing, cost.

---

## the shape of the timeline

- production runs, for years, without incident
- the line stops; staff move on or retire
- the material is needed again
- specifications and procedures are located, intact
- reliable reproduction does not follow from having them

---

## what the government's own reporting says

The U.S. Government Accountability Office's 2008 report on nuclear weapons
material stewardship is the primary source this deck stays inside. It
documents cost and schedule, not chemistry.

```notes
Point students at the reading before this slide, not after — GAO-08-593 is
the anchor citation for this entire week, and it's worth having open during
the seminar discussion, not just before it.
```

---

## the cost of reinstatement

Public reporting describes a reinstatement effort measured in years and in
tens of millions of dollars — the closest thing this course has to an
invoice for a capability that had to be rebuilt rather than simply looked
up.

---

## the question this week leaves for the seminar

At which point on this timeline could an outside auditor, reading only the
records available at that time, have flagged the risk?
