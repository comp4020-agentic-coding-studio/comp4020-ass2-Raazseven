# Policies and support

These policies apply to every assessment in SLOP6119 and are maintained by
the School of Institutional Memory in accordance with the University's
general coursework rules. Where a specific assessment brief states
different terms, the assessment brief governs for that assessment only.

## Attendance

Attendance at seminars is expected but not marked on a per-session basis.
A student who misses more than three seminars in a semester should contact
the convenor; the resulting conversation is recorded in the School's
attendance register in the manner set out in Schedule 4 of the Seminar
Participation Procedure.

## Extensions

Extension requests for the case dossier and the reproduction attempt are
assessed by the tutor against the University's standard extension grounds.
Requests for the seminar paper are assessed by the convenor. All requests
must be submitted before the original due date using the University's
extension request form, and should reference the relevant clause of the
Coursework Extensions Procedure.

## Academic integrity

Work submitted for this course must be the student's own, and every factual
and historical claim must be attributable to a source the student has
themselves checked. This requirement is not a formality: a fabricated or
unverifiable citation is treated, across every assessment in this course, as
more serious than an argument built honestly on weaker evidence. Suspected
breaches are handled under the University's Academic Integrity Policy and
referred to the Faculty integrity panel in the first instance.

## Accreditation

SLOP6119 is offered as an elective within the School of Institutional
Memory's postgraduate program and is accredited under the University's
standard coursework accreditation process. The program's most recent
accreditation review is on file with the Faculty office. Students seeking
recognition of this course toward an external qualification should consult
their home institution's credit transfer policy directly, as the School
does not hold delegated authority to make that assessment on their behalf.

## Support

Students seeking academic support should contact their tutor in the first
instance, then the convenor. Students seeking wellbeing, disability, or
financial support should contact the University's central student support
service, which sits outside the School and is not administered by this
course.
