## the opposite failure

Week 2 was an absent record. This week is the opposite: a complete one
that still doesn't transfer the [capability](/glossary/#capability) it
describes.

```notes
Say the opposite framing explicitly — the room will otherwise try to

```

---

## what the manual actually describes

A procedure manual can pass every audit — every step present, every
sign-off recorded — and still fail the moment someone who has never done
the thing tries to follow it cold. The manual describes what a competent
practitioner does. It was never written to teach the competence itself.

---

## two different claims

- "We have full records" — a claim about the **archive**
- "We can currently do this" — a claim about the **organisation**

These get confused constantly, usually by the people relying on the
records rather than the people who wrote them.

```notes
Ask who in the room has seen these two claims get swapped for each other
in something they've worked on — it's a fast way into the discussion.
```

## discussion

- What's the difference between "the manual is complete" and "the manual
  works"?
- Recall an audit you've seen pass. Did it check the archive, or the
  practice?
- Think of instructions you followed that turned out to need something
  unstated.
- If a record can be complete and still insufficient, what would a record
  that closed the gap even look like — is "more detail" the right axis to
  add it on, or the wrong one?
  {/* facilitate: this is the question left open — let it stand unresolved into the wrap-up */}

## activity: find the missing sentence

**Setup (5 min).** Recall a set of instructions you've followed that
turned out to need something the instructions didn't say — a recipe, an
assembly guide, an onboarding doc.

**Groups (20 min).** In groups of three, each person walks the group
through their example. Together, identify the exact sentence where the
gap was, and classify what kind of thing was missing: a judgement call, a
piece of context, a physical skill, a corrective for when it goes
slightly wrong.

**Share-back (15 min).** Tally the categories on the board across all
groups.

```notes
Push groups to name the *exact sentence*, not the general area — vague
answers here ("the instructions weren't detailed enough") are the
"more detail" trap the wrap-up is about to name.
```

---

## debrief

Which category dominated the board's tally — judgement calls, context,
physical skill, or corrective handling? Compare that against week 2's
categories from writing unwritable instructions. Same shape of gap, or a
different one?

---

## wrap-up

Completeness is a property of documents, not of practices — a full record
doesn't guarantee the capability behind it, and "more detail" isn't
automatically the fix. Week 4 gives you the sharpest version of this
pattern at institutional scale: a full record, a capability that stopped
anyway.
