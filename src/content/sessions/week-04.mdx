This week is the course's vertical slice: one case, worked end to end, in
the depth every other week gestures at. It stays entirely at the level of
institutional and documentary history — organisational timeline, funding,
staffing, cost — and does not describe, and will not be asked to describe,
any materials, process, or engineering detail belonging to the weapons
component involved. The case is here for what it shows about records and
capability, not for anything about the thing itself.

## The case, or the argument

FOGBANK is the U.S. government's name for a material used in a component of
certain nuclear weapons. Production stopped for years. When the material
was needed again, the specifications, procedures, and paperwork from the
original production run had all survived — and the material still could not
be reliably remade from them. Publicly available accounts converge on the
same institutional explanation: some part of what the original production
depended on had never been captured in the documentation that outlived it,
because the people who ran the line had never needed to write it down for
themselves. Reinstating production required years of renewed effort and a
substantial rebuilding cost, tracked in the government's own oversight
reporting rather than in the original production files.

Read against weeks 2 and 3: this is not a case of missing paperwork. The
paperwork was there. It is a case where the paperwork was never the whole
capability to begin with, and nobody found that out until the capability
was needed and the paperwork alone couldn't produce it.

## What we do in the seminar

Walk the deck for this week slide by slide as a group, then work backward:
at which point in the timeline could an outside auditor, reading only the
records available at that time, have flagged the risk that reinstatement
would be this hard? Compare answers before checking them against what
actually happened.
