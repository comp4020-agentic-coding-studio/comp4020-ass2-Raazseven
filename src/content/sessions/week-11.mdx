This week applies the course's method to a case still in progress, and it
stops short of a conclusion on purpose. The claim on this page, and
everything below it, presents a case and a counter-case and leaves them
there. Nothing on this page is the course's verdict, because the course
does not have one to give — the point of the last ten weeks was to build a
method sharp enough to use here, not to arrive already knowing what it
will find.

## The case, and the counter-case

**The case for a loss in progress.** Generative models can now produce
competent first drafts of writing, code, and analysis that a person used to
have to produce themselves in order to develop the underlying judgement.
Weeks 6 and 7 both describe how a capability can erode without anyone
deciding to erode it: automate the output well enough, and the practice
that used to build the underlying skill stops happening as often. If that
pattern holds here, the [tacit knowledge](/glossary/#tacit-knowledge)
built by doing the work yourself is exactly the kind of thing week 2 argues
doesn't survive in the output alone — and the output is increasingly all
that gets produced.

**The case against.** Every automation this course has studied changed what
skill was needed without necessarily reducing the total skill in the
system — Zuboff's argument is precisely that smart machines demand new
capabilities from the people who work with them, even as they remove old
ones. Editing, prompting, and judging model output at scale may be a real
capability being built right now, not a gap opening up. On this reading,
week 1's distinction cuts the other way: the record (what the model
produces) and the capability (what the person using it can actually do) are
just as separable in the optimistic direction as in the pessimistic one.

## What we do in the seminar

Split the room. Argue each side for the first half using only material from
weeks 1–10, cited by week number. Then argue against your own side for the
second half. No side is graded as the "correct" one, because this page
doesn't have one.

## One reading

[Zuboff (1988)](/readings/#zuboff-1988) — written about an earlier wave of
workplace automation, and the closest thing this syllabus has to a text
that already argues both sides of this week's question.

## A question left open

This whole page is the open question. Decide, if you want to, on your own
time — but notice which of the last ten weeks' cases you found yourself
reaching for first, and ask what that says about which side you were
already inclined to argue.
