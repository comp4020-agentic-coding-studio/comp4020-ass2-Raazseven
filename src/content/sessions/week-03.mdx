## The case, or the argument

Take the opposite failure from week 2: not an absent record, but a complete
one that still doesn't transfer the [capability](/glossary/#capability) it
describes. A procedure manual can pass every audit — every step present,
every sign-off recorded, nothing missing that a reviewer would ask for — and
still fail the moment someone who has never done the thing tries to follow
it cold. The manual describes what a competent practitioner does. It was
never written to teach the competence itself.

This week reframes "completeness" as a property of documents, not of
practices, and treats "we have full records" as a claim about the archive,
not a claim about what the organisation can currently do. The two claims
get confused constantly, usually by the people relying on the records
rather than the people who wrote them.

## What we do in the seminar

Bring a set of instructions you've followed that turned out to need
something the instructions didn't say — a recipe, an assembly guide, an
onboarding doc. Identify the exact sentence where the gap was, and what
kind of thing was missing: a judgement call, a piece of context, a physical
skill, a corrective for when it goes slightly wrong.
